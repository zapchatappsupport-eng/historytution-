Q.7. Briefly discuss the contributions of Bhakti saints to Indian society and culture.

Bhakti Saints had a major impact on Indian culture

  • Kabirdas’s compositions talked of one god and decry the rituals of both Hinduism and Islam which appealed to many people.
  • Ravidas too promoted the idea of equality and oneness of all, breaking down the wall of varna and jati.
  • Guru Nanak journeyed across regions, spreading the message of one god and equality of people.

Q.13. Highlight the extraordinary features of the blended form of performing art styles that existed in medieval Indian society.

Performing art styles in India developed under the patronage of the Royals with integration of foreign ideas and forms.

  • Sufis created Qwallis fusing local classical musical styles with their own which was performed along with dance.
  • Traditional storytelling was developed accompanied by song, dance and drama varying from region to region like puppetry in Rajasthan and Pattachitra in Odisha.
  • Emotions of every day life - Love, grief, pain, longing also entered the tradition of storytelling.
  • Music and dances saw a transition from being performed at temple to courts.
  • Composers like Tansen was influenced by sufism as well as composed songs of krishna and shiva.
  • The period also saw adaptation of instruments which are still used today like the sitar and tabla.

Q.22. What was the position of women from royal families in the royal court before the 12th century? Why did the status of women change after that?

Before 12th century, the Princes of India allowed women to attend their courts, without any veil.

But after invasions and military campaigns from 12th century onwards, women were imprisoned and sold as slaves in West and Central Asia. To protect themselves, women committed Jauhar too. As a result, women's public engagement and appearance declined.

Q.23. Highlight the steps taken by royalty and common masses in the Indian society to protect their women from external exposure to public places.

To protect their women from being enslaved or being sold, the Royalties and common masses reduced the appearance and engagement of women.

Practices such as the Purdah (veil) became widespread in certain region.

In North India, Veil became a part of attire to avoid public eyes.

Royal women were mostly secluded and kept hidden from the eyes of the commoners.
